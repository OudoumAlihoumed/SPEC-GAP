Title: Root Cause Analysis of the March 2025 Regional Fiber Backbone Outage

Abstract: On March 9, 2025, a metro fiber backbone serving 340,000 subscribers experienced a 4-hour 12-minute outage. This report documents the incident timeline, root cause, and remediation steps taken by the network operations center (NOC).

Methods: We reconstructed the incident from NOC ticket logs, SNMP trap history, and post-incident interviews with on-call engineers. Packet-loss and latency telemetry from 22 backbone nodes were reviewed for the six hours surrounding the outage. Root cause was assigned using the operator's standard five-whys escalation framework.

Results: The outage began when a contractor severed a buried fiber trunk during unrelated roadwork, taking the primary ring segment between Node 11 and Node 14 offline. Automatic protection switching should have rerouted traffic within 50 milliseconds, but a misconfigured BGP local-preference value on Node 14 caused the backup path to be deprioritized, delaying failover by 41 minutes. Full service was restored once on-call engineers manually corrected the routing policy and dispatched a splice crew. Total subscriber-minutes of downtime were estimated at 86.6 million.

Discussion: The incident exposed a gap between the documented protection-switching design and the deployed configuration on Node 14, which had drifted from the reference template during a prior maintenance window. The operator has since added automated configuration-drift detection to the change-management pipeline.

Conclusion: The outage was caused by a physical fiber cut combined with a configuration error that delayed automatic failover; corrective action has focused on configuration-drift monitoring rather than physical hardening alone.
