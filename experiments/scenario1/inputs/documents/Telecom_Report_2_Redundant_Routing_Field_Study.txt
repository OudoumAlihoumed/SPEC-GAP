Title: Redundant Routing Architectures for Metro Fiber Networks: A Comparative Field Study

Abstract: This study compares three redundant routing architectures deployed across nine metro fiber networks: ring protection switching, mesh with dynamic MPLS rerouting, and dual-homed spur topology. We measure failover time, cost per protected route-kilometer, and five-year incident rates.

Methods: Data were drawn from operator incident logs and maintenance records for nine metro networks over a five-year period (2019-2024). Failover time was measured as the interval between a simulated or real link failure and restoration of full traffic flow. Cost per protected route-kilometer included capital equipment and annual maintenance, normalized to 2023 dollars.

Results: Ring protection switching achieved a median failover time of 51 milliseconds at a cost of $38,000 per protected route-kilometer, the fastest and most expensive option. Mesh with dynamic MPLS rerouting achieved a median failover time of 210 milliseconds at $24,500 per route-kilometer. Dual-homed spur topology, used mainly for lower-tier access rings, achieved 1.8 seconds median failover at $9,200 per route-kilometer. Five-year major-incident rates were lowest for mesh architectures (0.6 incidents per 100 route-kilometers) despite their slower failover, because mesh topologies tolerated a wider range of simultaneous fault combinations without full service loss.

Discussion: The results suggest that failover speed and overall reliability are not the same property: ring architectures restore fastest from a single fault but are more brittle under compound failures, while mesh architectures trade some failover speed for tolerance of multiple simultaneous faults. Operators serving latency-sensitive traffic classes should weight failover time more heavily, while those prioritizing five-year uptime should weight incident rate.

Conclusion: No single redundant routing architecture dominates on all metrics; the choice should be driven by the traffic profile and fault-tolerance requirements of the specific network tier.
